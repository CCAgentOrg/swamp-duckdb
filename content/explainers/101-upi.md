# UPI 101: Understanding India's Unified Payments Interface

## What is UPI?

The **Unified Payments Interface (UPI)** is a real-time, bank-to-bank payment system developed by the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) that enables instant money transfers between any two bank accounts using a mobile platform. It is the flagship component of India's Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) and the world's largest real-time payment system by volume. [^1][^2]

Launched on **11 April 2016** by the then RBI Governor Dr. Raghuram Rajan, with 21 participating banks and just 373 transactions in its first month, UPI was designed to solve three fundamental problems that had kept digital payments fragmented in India: **interoperability, instant processing, and user convenience**. Instead of requiring users to share bank account numbers and IFSC codes (as with NEFT/RTGS) or load money into wallet silos (as with PPIs), UPI introduced the **Virtual Payment Address (VPA)** — a simple, human-readable identifier (e.g., `username@bankname`) that maps to any bank account. [^3][^4]

UPI is built on top of the existing **Immediate Payment Service (IMPS)** infrastructure, which provides the underlying 24×7 settlement rail between banks. UPI adds an abstraction layer — standardised APIs, VPAs, and a central switch operated by NPCI — that transforms IMPS from a technical banking tool into a consumer-friendly payment platform. [^5]

UPI processed **24,161.69 crore (241.6 billion) transactions** worth **₹314 lakh crore (~$3.7 trillion)** in FY2025-26, representing 30% year-on-year volume growth. It accounts for approximately **85% of all digital payment transaction volume** in India and **49% of the world's real-time payment transactions by volume**. [^6][^7]

### Key Milestones

- **April 2016**: UPI launched by NPCI with 21 banks, 373 transactions in the first month [^3]
- **August 2016**: Major banks begin offering UPI-enabled apps to customers [^8]
- **November 2016**: Demonetisation accelerates digital payment adoption; UPI volumes begin climbing sharply [^9]
- **December 2016**: BHIM (Bharat Interface for Money) app launched by the Government of India [^8]
- **2017**: PhonePe and Google Tez (later Google Pay) launch, driving mass consumer adoption [^10]
- **2018**: NPCI launches UPI 2.0 with mandate (autopay), invoice-in-box, and signed intent features [^8]
- **2020**: UPI transaction volumes cross ₹1 lakh crore per month for the first time [^11]
- **March 2022**: UPI 123PAY launched — enabling UPI payments via feature phones, IVR, and missed calls [^12]
- **September 2022**: UPI Lite launched — on-device wallet for small payments up to ₹2,000 (later raised to ₹5,000) without bank connectivity [^13]
- **2022**: RBI's Payment Vision 2025 outlines roadmap for UPI's international expansion [^14]
- **2023**: UPI goes live in **Bhutan** — first country outside India to accept UPI [^15]
- **2023**: NPCI introduces **credit-on-UPI** framework allowing transactions via RuPay credit cards and overdraft facilities [^8]
- **2024**: UPI launches in **France, UAE, Singapore, Sri Lanka, Nepal, Mauritius** [^15][^16]
- **2024**: NPCI begins rolling out **UPI 3.0** features including UPI Circle (delegated payments) and enhanced transaction limits [^17]
- **April 2026**: UPI celebrates 10 years with 703 banks live, record monthly volume of 2,264 crore transactions (March 2026) [^6]
- **2025**: UPI goes live in **Qatar** — Lulu Hypermarket becomes the first major retail chain to accept UPI in the country [^18]

## How UPI Works

### The UPI Architecture

UPI operates through a layered architecture involving multiple entities, each with defined roles:

**1. User Layer** — The customer uses a **UPI app** (Google Pay, PhonePe, Paytm, BHIM, or any bank's own app) to initiate or receive payments. The user must create a **Virtual Payment Address (VPA)** that is linked to one or more bank accounts.

**2. Payment Service Provider (PSP)** — The entity that provides the UPI app to the user (e.g., PhonePe, Google Pay). PSPs handle user onboarding, VPA creation, and the frontend interface. PSPs must be NPCI-certified and partner with a bank for settlement. [^5]

**3. Paying Bank / Remitter Bank** — The bank where the payer's account is held. This bank receives the payment request from the PSP, verifies the payer's identity and balance, and authenticates the transaction (typically via a UPI PIN).

**4. Receiving Bank / Beneficiary Bank** — The bank where the payee's account is held. This bank receives credit instructions through the UPI switch and credits the beneficiary's account.

**5. UPI Switch (NPCI)** — The central routing and settlement hub operated by NPCI. The switch processes transaction requests, routes them to the correct banks, performs validation checks, and facilitates settlement between banks. It is geo-redundant with **FIPS 140-2 Level 3 certified Hardware Security Modules (HSMs)**. [^5][^19]

**6. Settlement Layer (IMPS)** — The underlying IMPS infrastructure provides the actual fund settlement between banks. UPI transactions are settled using the existing IMPS settlement accounts that banks maintain with RBI.

### Transaction Flow

A typical P2P UPI payment follows this sequence:

1. **Payer initiates** — Opens UPI app, enters payee's VPA and amount
2. **PSP validates** — Checks VPA format, sends request to NPCI switch
3. **NPCI routes** — Verifies both VPAs exist, forwards debit request to payer's bank
4. **Payer's bank authenticates** — Prompts for UPI PIN; validates PIN and available balance
5. **NPCI instructs credit** — Forwards credit instruction to payee's bank
6. **Payee's bank credits** — Credits beneficiary account
7. **Settlement** — Inter-bank settlement happens through the IMPS settlement layer
8. **Confirmation** — Both parties receive real-time confirmation

The entire process typically completes in **under 2 seconds**. [^5][^20]

### Virtual Payment Address (VPA)

The VPA is UPI's defining innovation. Structured as `username@handle` (e.g., `rajesh@hdfcbank`, `priya@paytm`), it eliminates the need to share sensitive bank account numbers and IFSC codes. A single VPA can map to multiple bank accounts, and a user can create multiple VPAs. The VPA is verified by the PSP through a multi-factor process involving mobile number verification and bank account linkage. [^4]

### UPI Transaction Types

| Type | Description | Key Feature |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| **P2P (Person-to-Person)** | Send money between individuals | Uses VPA, mobile number, or account+IFSC |
| **P2M (Person-to-Merchant)** | Pay at merchant outlets | QR code or intent-based (payment link/push) |
| **P2B (Person-to-Business)** | Bill payments, tax, insurance | Invoice-based or intent-based |
| **Recurring (AutoPay)** | Automated periodic payments | Mandate with pre-defined limits |
| **Collect Request** | Request money from someone | Payee initiates, payer approves |
| **Scheduled** | Set future-dated payments | Available in newer UPI versions |

### Transaction Limits (as of 2026)

| Context | Per Transaction Limit | Notes |
|---------|----------------------|-------|
| Default UPI transaction | ₹1 lakh | Standard for most banks |
| Bank-defined limits | ₹1 lakh to ₹5 lakh | Banks may set higher limits for specific categories |
| Special categories (hospital, education, insurance) | Up to ₹5 lakh | RBI-approved enhanced limits [^21] |
| UPI Lite | ₹500 per transaction, ₹5,000 cumulative wallet | No PIN required |
| UPI 123PAY | ₹5,000 per transaction | Feature phone payments |

## Layers Classification (DPI Stack)

UPI operates within India's broader Digital Public Infrastructure stack. Using the India Stack framework, UPI spans multiple layers: [^26][^27]

### L1 — Physical Infrastructure Layer
- **Telecom networks**: Mobile internet connectivity (Jio's 2016 launch was pivotal for UPI adoption)
- **Smartphone penetration**: 750+ million smartphone users in India
- **Data centres**: NPCI's geo-redundant data centres with HSM modules

### L2 — Identity Layer
- **Aadhaar**: Used for bank account KYC and initial onboarding
- **Mobile number**: The primary device binding and 2FA factor for UPI
- **VPA**: UPI's own identity abstraction layer

### L3 — Payments Layer (UPI's Core)
- **UPI Switch**: NPCI-operated central routing and settlement hub
- **IMPS**: Underlying real-time gross settlement infrastructure
- **PSP Network**: Third-party apps providing UPI access (PhonePe, Google Pay, Paytm, etc.)
- **Bank Integration Layer**: 703 banks connected to UPI

### L4 — Data Layer
- **Transaction metadata**: Amount, parties, timestamp, device info
- **Bank statements**: Stored by individual banks
- **NPCI logs**: Settlement data and routing information (retained for regulatory compliance)

### L5 — Application/Service Layer
- **P2P transfers**: Consumer-to-consumer payments
- **P2M payments**: Merchant payments via QR codes
- **Bill payments**: Integration with Bharat BillPay
- **Recurring payments**: AutoPay mandates
- **Credit-on-UPI**: RuPay credit card and overdraft-linked payments

### L6 — Ecosystem Layer
- **International linkages**: UPI-PayNow (Singapore), UPI-Linx (UAE), and connections to Bhutan, Nepal, Sri Lanka, France, Mauritius, Qatar, Malaysia
- **Open API ecosystem**: nfinite.in developer platform for UPI integration
- **QR code standardisation**: NPCI's interoperable merchant QR code

### L7 — Governance Layer
- **RBI**: Overall regulatory authority under Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007
- **NPCI**: Operational governance, rule-making, dispute resolution
- **MeitY**: Policy and diplomatic coordination for international DPI exports
- **DPDPA compliance**: Data protection obligations under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023

## Regulatory Framework

### Primary Legislation

**Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007 (PSS Act)** — The foundational law governing all payment systems in India. It vests the RBI with the authority to regulate, authorise, and supervise payment systems. No entity can operate a payment system in India without RBI authorisation under Section 4 of the PSS Act. NPCI's UPI operates under this framework. [^28]

### Regulatory Bodies

| Body | Role |
|------|------|
| **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)** | Primary regulator; authorises UPI under PSS Act; issues circulars on security, limits, and consumer protection |
| **National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI)** | Operates UPI switch; sets technical standards; manages dispute redressal; onboards PSPs and banks |
| **Ministry of Electronics and IT (MeitY)** | Coordinates DPI policy; manages UPI's international expansion via NPCI International Payments Ltd (NIPL) |
| **Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C)** | Investigates and reports on digital payment fraud trends |

### Key Regulatory Frameworks

**1. RBI Master Directions on Digital Payments** — Sets comprehensive rules for UPI operation, including: [^28][^29]

- Customer liability framework for unauthorised transactions (zero liability if reported within 3 days, limited liability up to 7 days, no protection beyond 7 days for customer negligence)
- Transaction monitoring and fraud detection obligations on PSPs and banks
- Data storage and localisation requirements (all payment system data must be stored in India)
- Cybersecurity and audit requirements for payment system participants

**2. RBI's Payment Systems Vision 2025 (2022)** — Outlined the strategic roadmap including: [^14]

- UPI's international expansion through linkage with foreign real-time payment systems
- Enhancement of UPI's credit capabilities
- Integration with emerging use cases like account aggregator-based lending

**3. NPCI UPI Guidelines and Circulars** — NPCI issues operational circulars covering: [^30]

- PSP onboarding requirements and compliance
- Transaction limits and authentication standards
- QR code specifications for merchant payments
- UPI Lite and UPI 123PAY operational rules
- Dispute redressal procedures
- Device binding and fraud prevention requirements

**4. Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDPA)** — India's comprehensive data protection law. UPI transaction data is "personal data" under the DPDPA, and NPCI, PSPs, and banks are "Data Fiduciaries" with obligations around: [^31]

- Purpose limitation and consent for data processing
- Data principal rights (access, correction, erasure)
- Breach notification requirements
- Cross-border data transfer restrictions (relevant for international UPI transactions)

**5. NPCI UPI Information Security Compliance Framework (2025)** — Issued jointly by NPCI and KPMG, this framework establishes: [^19]

- Governance controls including designated CISOs for all ecosystem participants
- Cyber risk management and resilience requirements
- AI/ML-based fraud detection obligations
- Transaction velocity monitoring and anomaly detection

### International Regulatory Considerations

As UPI expands internationally, it encounters foreign regulatory frameworks including: [^14][^32]

- **Foreign exchange regulations**: Cross-border UPI transactions must comply with FEMA rules and RBI's foreign exchange management requirements
- **AML/KYC obligations**: International PSPs must meet both Indian and local anti-money laundering requirements
- **Data localisation**: UPI transaction data involving foreign jurisdictions raises complex data sovereignty questions
- **Supervisory oversight**: No single regulator has full oversight of a cross-border UPI transaction — creating potential compliance gaps

## Citizen Rights Analysis

### What UPI Gets Right

**1. Financial Inclusion**: UPI has been the single most powerful tool for bringing India's population into the formal financial system. Combined with PMJDY (Jan Dhan) accounts and mobile phone penetration, UPI enables anyone with a bank account and a phone to send and receive money. Women, rural populations, and informal economy workers — historically excluded from formal banking — can now participate in digital payments. [^33]

**2. Zero MDR (Merchant Discount Rate)**: RBI mandates zero MDR on UPI merchant payments up to ₹2,000 per transaction. This means small merchants accept digital payments without paying interchange fees — a significant departure from card-based systems where MDR typically ranges from 1-3%. This policy is a cornerstone of UPI's mass adoption. [^34]

**3. Interoperability**: UPI is fundamentally interoperable — any UPI app can send money to any other UPI app, and any bank account linked to UPI can receive payments from any PSP. This prevents vendor lock-in and gives consumers genuine choice of app. [^35]

**4. 24×7 Availability**: Unlike NEFT (which used to operate in batches) or bank transfers limited to business hours, UPI is available 24 hours a day, 365 days a year, including on bank holidays. This has made it a de facto replacement for cash in everyday transactions. [^1]

**5. Instant Settlement**: Unlike credit card payments that can take 1-3 business days to settle, UPI credits the recipient's account almost instantly. For gig workers, small businesses, and anyone relying on timely payments, this is transformative. [^1]

### Concerns and Risks

**1. Concentration Risk**: While UPI is interoperable at the protocol level, the market is heavily concentrated. PhonePe and Google Pay together handle ~80% of UPI volume. [^23] If either app experiences an outage or security breach, it can disrupt payments for a large portion of India's population. There is no meaningful regulatory mechanism to address this concentration.

**2. Social Engineering Fraud**: The majority of UPI fraud is not technical but social — fraudsters trick victims into approving payments by impersonating authorities, creating fake payment links, or using screen-sharing apps. In FY2025-26, ₹805 crore was lost to such frauds across 10.64 lakh cases. [^24][^25] The system design places the authentication burden on the user, making social engineering the primary attack vector.

**3. Low Recovery Rates**: Only ~6% of stolen UPI funds are recovered. [^25] This means the fraud protection framework, while theoretically strong (zero liability within 3 days), is practically weak for most victims. Banks often reject claims citing "customer negligence" as the reason for unauthorised transactions.

**4. Feature Phone Exclusion**: While UPI 123PAY and UPI Lite address some accessibility gaps, the primary UPI experience requires a smartphone. India still has 250-300 million feature phone users who cannot fully participate in the UPI ecosystem. [^12]

**5. Data Exhaust**: Every UPI transaction generates metadata — who paid whom, when, where (via device GPS and merchant location), and for what (via merchant category codes). This data, while technically anonymised, can be used to build detailed profiles of citizens' financial behaviour, social connections, and consumption patterns. The implications under the DPDPA are still being worked out. [^36]

## Privacy Implications

### Who Has Access to Your UPI Data?

A single UPI transaction involves multiple entities, each with access to different parts of the data: [^36][^37]

| Entity | Data Access |
|--------|------------|
| **PSP (PhonePe/Google Pay)** | Transaction history, VPA, device ID, app usage patterns, location data |
| **Payer's Bank** | Account holder details, balance, full transaction history |
| **Payee's Bank** | Beneficiary account details, incoming transaction history |
| **NPCI** | Routing data, settlement amounts, VPA mappings (no direct consumer data) |
| **Government (on request)** | Can access data through RBI/court orders under PSS Act provisions |

### Key Privacy Concerns

**1. PSP Data Harvesting**: PSPs like Google Pay and PhonePe collect extensive data beyond what is necessary for payment processing — including app usage patterns, device identifiers, and in some cases, transaction categorisation. This data can be used for targeted advertising and credit scoring. The DPDPA requires consent for such secondary use, but consent in practice is often bundled with app terms that users do not meaningfully negotiate. [^36]

**2. Transaction Profiling**: Banks and PSPs can build detailed financial profiles from UPI transaction patterns — merchant visits, subscription payments, peer groups, lifestyle indicators. There is no regulatory limit on how long this data can be retained or how it can be used internally. [^36]

**3. International Data Transfers**: With UPI expanding to 8+ countries, transaction data increasingly crosses borders. While NPCI and participating banks are required to store core payment data in India, the metadata and analytics may be processed elsewhere. The DPDPA restricts cross-border data transfers but includes exemptions that may apply to financial data. [^32]

**4. No Right to Data Portability in Practice**: While the DPDPA provides for data portability rights, there is no practical mechanism for a UPI user to download, port, or delete their transaction history from all involved entities simultaneously. [^31]

**5. Surveillance Potential**: As highlighted by the CSIS analysis of UPI's 10th anniversary, systems designed for rapid economic participation can also enable large-scale extraction of behavioural data. The combination of UPI transaction data with Aadhaar identity data and DigiLocker document storage creates a comprehensive digital profile of citizens. [^37]

## Safeguards

### For Consumers

**1. Zero Liability Protection**: Under RBI's framework, customers are entitled to zero liability for unauthorised UPI transactions if: [^28]
- The loss is reported to the bank within **3 working days** of receiving the transaction alert
- The unauthorised transaction is not due to the customer's own negligence

If reported between 4-7 working days, liability is limited. Beyond 7 days, the bank may determine liability based on investigation.

**2. UPI PIN Protection**: The UPI PIN is known only to the user — it is never stored on the PSP's servers or transmitted to NPCI. Even if a PSP app is compromised, the PIN cannot be extracted. [^5]

**3. Device Binding**: UPI transactions are device-bound — a registered mobile device must be used for authentication. Changing devices requires re-registration, providing a layer of protection against credential theft. [^5]

**4. Transaction Alerts**: Banks send SMS/email alerts for every UPI transaction. Monitoring these alerts is the primary way to detect unauthorised transactions quickly and claim zero liability protection. [^28]

**5. Biometric Lock (Aadhaar)**: While primarily an AePS safeguard, locking Aadhaar biometrics through UIDAI's biometric lock service prevents unauthorised access to any biometric-authenticated payment service. [^38]

**6. UPI Lite**: For small payments, UPI Lite provides an on-device wallet with a ₹5,000 cumulative limit. Since transactions below ₹500 require no PIN, the liability is capped at the wallet balance. [^13]

### For the Ecosystem

**1. NPCI AI/ML Fraud Detection**: NPCI operates a centralised anti-fraud solution using AI/ML for real-time risk scoring and anomaly detection. This system analyses transaction patterns across the entire UPI network. [^19]

**2. Geo-Redundant Infrastructure**: NPCI's data centres are geo-redundant with FIPS 140-2 Level 3 certified HSMs, ensuring both availability and security. [^19]

**3. Information Security Framework**: The 2025 NPCI-KPMG Information Security Compliance Framework mandates designated CISOs, periodic security audits, and governance controls for all UPI ecosystem participants. [^19]

**4. Data Localisation**: Under RBI requirements, all UPI transaction data must be stored in India. This prevents foreign access to payment metadata. [^28]

**5. UPI Circle (UPI 3.0)**: This feature allows a primary user to delegate payment capabilities to a secondary user (e.g., parent to child) with transaction limits set by the primary user. This is designed for families and provides controlled access rather than full account access. [^17]

## Complaints & Grievance Redressal

### Escalation Ladder

UPI provides a multi-tier grievance redressal mechanism: [^39][^40]

**Level 1: In-App Support**
- Raise a complaint within the UPI app (PhonePe, Google Pay, BHIM, etc.)
- Provide transaction ID, date, amount, and description of issue
- PSP must respond within **10 working days** (5 working days for failed transactions)

**Level 2: PSP/Bank Level**
- If the PSP does not resolve the issue, escalate to the PSP's partner bank
- For issues with the receiving bank, contact them directly
- Use the bank's customer care or branch channels

**Level 3: NPCI Dispute Redressal**
- File a complaint through NPCI's online portal at [npcisupport.org.in](https://www.npcisupport.org.in)
- Call the UPI toll-free helpline: **1800-120-1740**
- Use the NPCI complaint tracker at [npci.org.in/complaint-status](https://www.npci.org.in/complaint-status)
- Upload proof of transaction (bank statement showing the debit)

**Level 4: RBI Integrated Ombudsman**
- If NPCI does not resolve the issue within 30 days, or if the resolution is unsatisfactory, file a complaint with the RBI Integrated Ombudsman
- Online portal: [cms.rbi.org.in](https://cms.rbi.org.in)
- Must be filed within 1 year of the complaint/transaction

**Level 5: DigiSaathi**
- NPCI's AI-powered chatbot at [npci.org.in/digisaathi](https://www.npci.org.in/digisaathi) for UPI-related queries
- Not a complaint mechanism, but provides guidance on grievance processes

### Known Gaps in Grievance Redressal

- **Tracking difficulty**: Multiple studies have found that complaint-tracking mechanisms in UPI apps are difficult to use, particularly for first-time users and those with low digital literacy [^39]
- **Asymmetric power**: PSPs (Google, PhonePe) have vastly more resources than individual complainants
- **No independent audit**: There is no publicly available data on resolution rates or average resolution times
- **Financial literacy gap**: Many users are unaware of their rights under the zero liability framework, or do not know how to escalate beyond the PSP level

- **Data localisation conflicts**: Cross-border data flows required for international UPI may conflict with India's data localisation requirements
- **Consumer protection gaps**: A user in France sending money via UPI may not have the same recourse mechanisms as a domestic Indian user

## UPI Versions

### UPI 1.0 (2016)
- Core P2P and P2M payments via VPA
- QR code payments
- Collection requests
- Basic account-to-account transfers

### UPI 2.0 (2018)
- **Mandate (AutoPay)**: Recurring payments with pre-defined limits
- **Signed Intent & Invoice**: QR code-based bill presentment with pre-filled payment details
- **OTP-based Authentication**: Alternative to UPI PIN for specific use cases

### UPI 123PAY (March 2022)
- Designed for **feature phone users** and those without internet
- Four payment modes: IVR, missed call, app functionality in feature phones, and proximity sound-based payments
- Enables UPI payments without a smartphone [^12]

### UPI Lite (September 2022)
- **On-device wallet** for small payments
- No internet connectivity required for transactions below ₹500
- Cumulative wallet limit: ₹5,000 (raised from ₹2,000 in 2024)
- No UPI PIN required for transactions below ₹500 [^13]

### UPI 3.0 (2024 onwards)
- **UPI Circle**: Delegated payment — primary user can set up secondary users (e.g., parents for children, employers for employees) with controlled spending limits
- **Enhanced transaction limits**: Up to ₹5 lakh for specific categories (hospitals, education, insurance)
- **Biometric payments**: Integration with device biometrics (fingerprint, face) as authentication alternatives
- **Conversational payments**: AI-assisted payment discovery and initiation

### Credit-on-UPI (2023 onwards)
- Allows UPI payments through **RuPay credit cards** instead of only debit/savings accounts
- Also supports **overdraft facility-linked UPI payments**
- Opens UPI to higher-value transactions and credit use cases [^8]

## Summary

UPI has been the defining story of India's DPI journey — growing from 373 transactions in April 2016 to 66 crore daily transactions a decade later. It is the world's largest real-time payment system, accounting for 49% of global real-time payment volume. Its design principles — interoperability, instant settlement, zero MDR, and open APIs — have become a template for digital payment systems worldwide. [^6][^37]

However, the system faces structural challenges. **Fraud losses exceeding ₹3,600 crore over five years** with only 6% recovery rates expose the gap between policy and practice. [^24][^25] Market concentration among two PSPs creates systemic risk. [^23] International expansion, while strategically important, outpaces the regulatory frameworks needed to govern cross-border payments effectively. [^32]

The DPDPA, enacted in 2023, provides a rights-based data protection framework that applies to UPI data, but its implementation is still evolving. Citizens' right to data portability, correction, and erasure exists in law but not yet in practice for the UPI ecosystem. [^31]

**For consumers**: UPI is remarkably convenient and mostly safe — but safety depends on user vigilance. Monitor transaction alerts immediately. Never share UPI PIN or OTP. Use UPI Lite for small, everyday payments to cap exposure. Know your zero-liability rights and the escalation ladder from PSP → Bank → NPCI → RBI Ombudsman.

**For policy**: The next decade of UPI needs to address concentration risk (perhaps through PSP market share caps), strengthen fraud recovery mechanisms (mandatory insurance funds for digital payment fraud), and build a genuinely cross-border regulatory framework before UPI's international expansion outpaces its governance.
